DATA LOG: OpenSim joint contact forces during treadmill walking, NIH ACL cohort
=============================================================================
Files: x-train.csv, y-train.csv, x-test.csv, y-test.csv (comma separated, one header row).
One row = one stride of the RIGHT leg. Row i of x-*.csv and row i of y-*.csv are the same stride
(same subject, block, stride_in_block). x = inputs (what a wearable or a gait lab measures), y = outputs
(what an OpenSim musculoskeletal simulation produces from those inputs). The modelling goal is to predict y from x.

1. WHERE THE DATA COME FROM
---------------------------
Cohort: 15 adults with a history of anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction (10 women, 5 men;
mass 56-91 kg; height 1.52-1.83 m), walking on an instrumented treadmill in the NIH ACL study. Each subject
walked 3 minutes at each of three belt speeds: 1.0, 1.25 and 1.5 m/s (Subject02 walked 1.4 m/s instead of 1.5).
Motion capture (marker trajectories) and force plates under each belt were recorded together.
From each 3-minute trial, three 1-minute blocks were taken and 5 consecutive strides were extracted from each
block, so a trial contributes up to 15 strides and a subject up to 45 (Subject01 and 02 have an extra trial, 50;
Subject14 has one unusable trial, 30). Total 670 strides from 134 blocks.

Processing (OpenSim 4.6, pipeline by K. Morgan, run unchanged apart from one static-trial fix for Subject12):
  - Model: Rajagopal 2016 full-body musculoskeletal model (Lai-Uhlrich 2023 update), scaled to each subject
    from a standing static trial; muscle strength scaled with mass and height (Handsfield 2014).
  - Inverse Kinematics (IK): joint angles from the marker trajectories.
  - Residual Reduction (RRA): small adjustments so model kinematics and measured ground reaction forces are
    dynamically consistent.
  - Static Optimisation (SO): muscle forces that reproduce the joint moments, minimising the sum of squared
    activations.
  - Joint Reaction Analysis (JRA): the force transmitted across each joint, given muscle and external forces.
  Quality was checked against the Hicks et al. 2015 recommendations (marker errors, residual forces, reserve
  actuators, muscle saturation). Strides from blocks that failed those checks are EXCLUDED from these files
  (see section 5).

2. THE GAIT CYCLE GRID (the "_tNNN" suffix)
-------------------------------------------
Every curve column is sampled at 101 equally spaced points of the gait cycle, t000 ... t100, where t000 is
right heel strike and t100 is the next right heel strike (so t100 of one stride is t000 of the next).
Heel strike and toe-off were detected from the vertical force on the right belt (threshold 20 N, forces
low-pass filtered at 6 Hz). Time is normalised, not absolute: a stride lasts about 1.1 s on average, and
stance (foot on the ground) ends at about 62 % of the cycle, swing is the remaining 38 %. Because every stride
is on the same grid, strides can be averaged, compared and modelled as curves (functional data).

3. COLUMNS COMMON TO ALL FOUR FILES (identifiers and scalar inputs)
------------------------------------------------------------------
subject          NIH_ACL_Subject01 ... Subject15. Identifier; NEVER an input to the model (see section 6).
block            trial and block, e.g. Walking125_b2 = 1.25 m/s trial, block 2 (Walking10 = 1.0 m/s,
                 Walking15 = 1.5 m/s, Walking14_B14 = 1.4 m/s, Subject02 only). Identifier.
stride_in_block  1-5, the stride's position within its 5-stride block. Identifier.
belt_speed_mps   treadmill belt speed in metres per second: 1.0, 1.25, 1.4 or 1.5. A real input.
mass_kg          body mass in kilograms, measured from the force plates during the static trial. Input.
height_m         standing height in metres, from the lab sheet. Input.

4. THE CURVE COLUMNS
--------------------
4a. Inputs, x-*.csv: 8 curves x 101 points = 808 columns, in this order.
  grf_vertical_BW_t000..t100         vertical ground reaction force under the right foot, in body weights
                                      (force / (mass x 9.80665)). ~0 in swing, two humps in stance
                                      (loading ~1.1 BW near 15 %, push-off near 50 %).
  grf_anteroposterior_BW_t000..t100  fore-aft ground reaction force, body weights. Positive = forward
                                      (propulsive); negative = braking. Braking early stance (~-0.17 BW near
                                      12 %), propulsion late stance (~+0.18 BW near 53 %).
  grf_mediolateral_BW_t000..t100     side-to-side ground reaction force, body weights, right foot. Negative =
                                      medial (toward the body midline). Small, about -0.08 BW at most.
  hip_flexion_deg_t000..t100         right hip flexion angle, degrees. Positive = flexion (thigh forward).
                                      ~+17 at heel strike, ~-22 (extension) near 53 %, back to +17 in swing.
  hip_adduction_deg_t000..t100       right hip adduction, degrees. Positive = adduction (thigh toward midline).
  hip_rotation_deg_t000..t100        right hip axial rotation, degrees. Positive = internal rotation.
  knee_flexion_deg_t000..t100        right knee flexion, degrees. Positive = flexion. ~4 at heel strike,
                                      small flexion wave in early stance, ~67 peak in swing near 73 %.
  ankle_angle_deg_t000..t100         right ankle, degrees. Positive = dorsiflexion (toes up), negative =
                                      plantarflexion. Dorsiflexion peak ~+12 near 49 %, plantarflexion ~-13
                                      at push-off/toe-off near 66 %.
  The GRFs come straight from the force plates (filtered, normalised). The angles are the OpenSim IK output,
  i.e. they already depend on the scaled model; they are what a gait lab would report as joint kinematics.
  Angles follow the sign conventions of the Rajagopal model coordinates (hip_flexion_r, hip_adduction_r,
  hip_rotation_r, knee_angle_r, ankle_angle_r).

4b. Outputs, y-*.csv: 3 curves x 101 points = 303 columns.
  hip_jcf_BW_t000..t100     right hip joint contact force (resultant), body weights
  knee_jcf_BW_t000..t100    right knee (tibiofemoral) joint contact force (resultant), body weights
  ankle_jcf_BW_t000..t100   right ankle (talocrural) joint contact force (resultant), body weights
  "Joint contact force" (JCF) = the total force pressing the two bones of a joint together: external load plus
  the compressive effect of all muscles spanning the joint. Muscles are why it is several body weights during
  walking even though the GRF is about 1 BW. Each value is the MAGNITUDE (Euclidean norm) of the 3-D force vector
  reported by OpenSim JRA on the child body (femur, tibia, talus) in that body's frame; the three components are
  not in these files. Typical mean curve: hip peaks 3.6 BW (14 %) and 3.9 BW (49 %); knee 2.9 BW (13 %) and
  4.0 BW (45 %); ankle 2.7 BW (29 %) and 5.5 BW (49 %); all fall to 0.3-0.6 BW in swing. Across strides the
  peaks range roughly 3-7 BW (hip, knee) and 4.5-8.5 BW (ankle). These are model-based estimates, not
  measurements; published instrumented-implant and model values for walking lie in these ranges.
  The JCFs are a deterministic function of the simulation inputs (kinematics, GRFs, scaled model, muscle
  model). The GP emulator approximates that function from data so that OpenSim need not be run.

5. WHAT WAS EXCLUDED, AND WHY (science flags)
---------------------------------------------
50 of the 670 strides are not in these files because their simulations failed the quality checks:
  Subject08, 1.5 m/s, all 15 strides: IK failure, shank marker error 44 mm RMS, implausible knee/ankle angles.
  Subject06, 1.5 m/s, all 15 strides: soleus at 100 % activation on both sides, ankle reserve 34 %, ankle JCF
                                      9-10 BW (the optimiser could not balance the ankle moment with muscles).
  Subject10, 1.5 m/s, all 15 strides: soleus and gastrocnemius saturated, ankle JCF 9.4 BW.
  Subject07, 1.5 m/s block 3, 5 strides: marker spike of 3.5 m, ankle tracking error 15 degrees.
Note that these are all at the fastest speed, so the files have fewer 1.5 m/s strides (160) than 1.0 (230) or
1.25 (210) m/s strides. Subject14's 1.25 m/s block 2 file was truncated at 4 s and was never simulated.
Subject12's static trial had an unlabelled pelvis marker; the pipeline's static-window selection was fixed so
that it uses a window where every marker is labelled, and Subject12 was re-run. Its right-leg results pass the
checks and are included.

6. TRAIN / TEST SPLIT: BY SUBJECT, NOT BY STRIDE
------------------------------------------------
Train: 12 subjects (02, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15), 480 strides
       (speeds: 1.0 m/s 180, 1.25 m/s 165, 1.4 m/s 20, 1.5 m/s 115).
Test:  3 subjects (01, 03, 12), 140 strides (1.0 m/s 50, 1.25 m/s 45, 1.5 m/s 45).
The three test subjects were drawn at random (numpy default_rng seed 2026) from the 15; every stride of a
test subject is in the test set and none in the training set.
Why by subject: the 5 strides of one block, and the 45 strides of one subject, are near-duplicates of each
other (same person, same speed, consecutive strides). A random split of strides puts near-copies of each test
stride in the training set, and the model only has to interpolate within a person. We measured this leakage:
knee RMSE 0.11 BW with a random stride split versus 0.22 BW with the subject split. The scientific question is
whether JCF can be predicted for a NEW person, so the subject split is the honest one. For model selection,
use leave-one-subject-out cross-validation within the 12 training subjects; use the 3 test subjects once, at
the end, to report performance. Never use the subject identifier as a feature.
The 1.4 m/s speed exists only in training (Subject02), so test predictions at that speed are not evaluated.

7. WHAT TO EXPECT AND HOW TO SCORE
----------------------------------
Baselines on the test subjects (RMSE over all 140 x 101 points, body weights): predicting the training mean
curve of the same speed gives hip 0.42, knee 0.29, ankle 0.38. A space-time Gaussian process on these inputs
gives about hip 0.29-0.33, knee 0.19-0.23, ankle 0.23-0.26. Most of the remaining error is subject-specific
and sits in early stance (20-30 % of the cycle), where the inputs do not determine the JCF.
Report at least: RMSE and MAPE of the curves, error of the peak value, and, for probabilistic models, CRPS
and the coverage of the 95 % prediction interval (how often the true curve lies inside it; should be 95 %).
Units: body weights for forces, degrees for angles, percent of gait cycle for time.
