DATA LOG: columns of x-train.csv, y-train.csv, x-test.csv, y-test.csv
=====================================================================
One row = one stride of the right leg (heel strike to the next heel strike) of one subject walking on a
treadmill. Row i of x-*.csv and row i of y-*.csv are the same stride. x = inputs, y = outputs.
Train: 480 strides, 12 subjects. Test: 140 strides, 3 other subjects.

IDENTIFIER COLUMNS (first 3 columns of every file; not inputs)
---------------------------------------------------------------
subject          NIH_ACL_Subject01 ... NIH_ACL_Subject15
block            trial and block, e.g. Walking125_b2 = the 1.25 m/s trial, block 2
                 (Walking10 = 1.0 m/s, Walking125 = 1.25 m/s, Walking15 = 1.5 m/s, Walking14_B14 = 1.4 m/s).
                 A block is 5 consecutive strides taken from one minute of a 3-minute trial.
stride_in_block  1 to 5, position of the stride within its block

SCALAR INPUT COLUMNS (columns 4 to 6 of every file)
---------------------------------------------------
belt_speed_mps   treadmill belt speed, m/s: 1.0, 1.25, 1.4 or 1.5
mass_kg          body mass, kg (from the force plates during the standing trial)
height_m         standing height, m

CURVE COLUMNS: the "_tNNN" suffix
---------------------------------
Each curve is sampled at 101 points of the gait cycle, t000 = heel strike, t100 = next heel strike, so NNN is
the percent of the gait cycle. Stance (foot on the belt) is about 0 to 62 %, swing about 62 to 100 %.

INPUT CURVES, x-*.csv (8 curves x 101 points = 808 columns, in this order)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
grf_vertical_BW_t000 ... t100         vertical ground reaction force under the right foot, in body weights
                                       (force divided by mass x 9.81). Zero in swing, two humps in stance.
grf_anteroposterior_BW_t000 ... t100  fore-aft ground reaction force, body weights. Positive = forward
                                       (propulsion, late stance), negative = braking (early stance).
grf_mediolateral_BW_t000 ... t100     side-to-side ground reaction force, body weights. Negative = toward
                                       the body midline. Small.
hip_flexion_deg_t000 ... t100         right hip flexion angle, degrees. Positive = thigh forward.
hip_adduction_deg_t000 ... t100       right hip adduction angle, degrees. Positive = thigh toward the midline.
hip_rotation_deg_t000 ... t100        right hip rotation angle, degrees. Positive = internal rotation.
knee_flexion_deg_t000 ... t100        right knee flexion angle, degrees. Positive = bent knee. About 5 at
                                       heel strike, about 65 at the swing peak.
ankle_angle_deg_t000 ... t100         right ankle angle, degrees. Positive = dorsiflexion (toes up),
                                       negative = plantarflexion (toes down, push-off).
The forces are measured by the force plates. The angles are the joint kinematics computed by OpenSim
inverse kinematics from the motion-capture markers.

OUTPUT CURVES, y-*.csv (3 curves x 101 points = 303 columns)
-----------------------------------------------------------
hip_jcf_BW_t000 ... t100     right hip joint contact force, body weights
knee_jcf_BW_t000 ... t100    right knee joint contact force, body weights
ankle_jcf_BW_t000 ... t100   right ankle joint contact force, body weights
A joint contact force (JCF) is the total force pressing the two bones of a joint together: the external load
plus the compression from the muscles crossing the joint. It is 3 to 6 body weights in walking. Each value is
the magnitude of the 3-D force computed by OpenSim (joint reaction analysis), not a measurement.

TRAIN / TEST SPLIT
------------------
The split is by SUBJECT: 3 of the 15 subjects (01, 03, 12), chosen at random, are the test set; all of their
strides are in x-test/y-test and none in the training files. The other 12 subjects are the training set.
Reason: strides of the same subject are near-copies of each other, so a random split of strides would put
near-copies of every test stride into training and overstate accuracy. The goal is to predict the JCF of a
person the model has never seen. Never use the subject column as an input.
